The ocean, right now

There is no single definition of a marine heatwave. Different thresholds, baselines and minimum durations answer different questions, and which one is appropriate is an open argument that this symposium is interested in rather than trying to close. The working definition we use is the qualitative one the field already shares: a prolonged, discrete, anomalously warm water event, describable by its duration, intensity, rate of onset and decline, and spatial extent.

One quantitative framework has nonetheless become a common language: the hierarchical definition and category scheme of Hobday and colleagues, which measures events against a 30-year baseline and a 90th-percentile threshold. Its value is less that it is uniquely correct than that it lets results from different oceans be compared at all. The map below shows what the world's oceans look like today under that framework.
